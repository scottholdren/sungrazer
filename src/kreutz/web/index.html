how to read a candidate
Open a card and play the animation. Watch the bottom row (the last 8 frames stacked along the track's motion) and the ring:
  • Comet: a compact dot that sits inside the ring column after column, usually brightening to the right. Because the stack is aligned on the motion, a real mover stays put in the ring.
  • Noise or structure: an empty ring, different mush each column, a bright thing drifting across the ring, a bar or streak through the tile (streamer edge, star trail), or a dot that lasts two or three columns and is gone.
  • Middle row = that single frame's significance map (a faint comet flickers there; normal). Top row = raw frame (only bright comets show).
A comet (Oct 24–25, 57σ, later reported on LASCO C2): a dot on the ticks in every column, brightening.
Typical noise (a 3-hit, 7σ candidate): nothing steady in the ring in the bottom row.
