# Review of 16b208e (detector + scoring)

Overall: the structure is right and the code is readable. Nothing here needs
rewriting. The items below are ordered by expected effect on the two numbers
that matter: precision at 3-4 hits, and lead time.

## 1. Tentative tracks die on a single missed window (recall + latency)

track.py, Tracker.update: a track that is not yet a candidate is dropped on
its first miss (`tr.misses > (MAX_MISSES if tr.candidate else 0)`), and
`consecutive` resets. So reaching 3 hits requires 3 consecutive windows with
a peak above 5 sigma. A faint comet hovering at 5-6 sigma (0075 is probably
this) will keep getting to 2 and restarting. A dropped off-pointing frame
has the same effect on every tentative track in the field.

Change: allow one miss before candidacy (3 hits within 4 windows), keep
MAX_MISSES=2 after. Then re-score and look specifically at whether 0075
appears and whether the @3-hit lead on the faint ones (0079) improves.

## 2. Rejection is recomputed on every update and can flip a real comet to rejected

Tracker.update recomputes `tr.rejected` after each hit. The glare-zone rule
uses the median distance of all hits from the Sun, so a comet that is valid
at 3-4 hits becomes "rejected:glare-zone" once it has spent enough frames
inside 320 px. Same risk for "extended" once a tail develops. For the race
this doesn't matter (you submit at 3-4 hits), but check score.py: if a track
is matched to a report only when its final status is unrejected, these
comets count as missed and the scorecard lies.

Change: record status at each hit count (reached[k] -> (t, status_at_k))
and score on status at the submission threshold, not the final status. A
rejection that arrives after the track was already submittable should be
logged, never applied retroactively.

## 3. GLARE_RADIUS=320 px is a July number

Comment says confirmed CCOR-1 comets were never reported closer than 392 px.
Solar radius is ~49 px, so 320 px is ~6.5 R_sun. Kreutz comets brighten
toward perihelion and some are followed much closer than that; whether
hunters report them that close depends on season and brightness. The
envelope fix (per-month closest-approach distance from the answer key)
should subsume this rule. Keep the glare rejection only as "median distance
< envelope minimum for this month", not a fixed 320.

## 4. The scorecard's "lead" ignores publication and processing latency

score.py computes lead as frame_time of the Nth frame minus the human post
time. Real lead is (time the Nth FITS appears on SWPC + time to fetch +
time to run the window) minus the post time. If NOAA publishes a frame
20 minutes after exposure, every lead in the table is 20 minutes
optimistic, and the @3-hit wins on 0077 (-0.1 h) and 0078 (-0.3 h) are
losses. Measure it: the live fetcher should log, for each frame, the
HTTP Last-Modified (or first-seen time) alongside obs time, and the
scorecard should add the measured median lag.

## 5. Integer shift rounding at half resolution

stack.py shifts by `int(round(v*dt))` half-res px. At dt=7 frames and
velocity step 0.5 px/frame the worst-case error is ~1.75 full-res px =
~1 half-res px, so a point source still lands in the right half-res pixel
most of the time. Fine for N=8. If N is ever raised or the grid step
coarsened, this starts smearing. Leave it; just noting the constraint.

## 6. Peak position precision for submissions

Peaks come from the half-res map (coords_from_ds adds 0.5). Hunters quote
integer pixels and the convention is verified only to about a pixel, so
this is adequate, but for the report block do a centroid on the full-res
significance map in a 5x5 box around each hit rather than quoting the
half-res centre. Cheap, and it removes a systematic +0.5.

## 7. Velocity grid is a box in (vx, vy)

velocity.py builds the box covering all confirmed velocities plus margin.
Kreutz velocities cluster along a band (direction correlates with position
in the field and season), so most of the box is wasted compute and, worse,
it admits velocities no comet has. The per-month envelope fix addresses the
rejection side; also prune the stacking grid to velocities within the
envelope so the stacker never produces those peaks in the first place.
Fewer peaks per window -> fewer chance 3-hit chains.

## 8. Pure-chance 3-hit chains are a measurable floor

With MAX_PEAKS=400, a 6 px match radius and 672 windows/week, the expected
number of 3-hit chains from uncorrelated noise alone is on the order of
tens per week. Precision at 3 hits can't exceed roughly 8/(8+that) no
matter how good the structured-noise rules get. The levers are MAX_PEAKS
(or a higher PEAK_THRESHOLD at 3 hits specifically), the velocity pruning
in item 7, and requiring velocity consistency across the 3 hits tighter
than MATCH_DV=2. Compute the floor explicitly in score.py by running the
tracker on time-shuffled peaks; it tells you when you've hit it.

## 9. Small things

- coords.py: October orientation (NROT90=2, YAWFLIP=0) was verified against
  unreviewed hunter reports with a peak at col -1. That's the 0-vs-1 question
  again. Resolve it before the first submission by measuring a star in
  Sungrazer's web tool and comparing.
- frames.py skips binned campaign frames (_0C). Correct, but log when a
  campaign is running so a gap in the live watcher is explained.
- Tracker.finish only returns candidates; tentative tracks that reached 2
  hits are discarded with no trace. Keep a count per window for the status
  page; it's the cheapest health signal that the stacker is alive.
- MAX_ELONGATION=3 with a 13x13 half-res box: the wider-box fix is right.
  Also consider rejecting on the newest hit's elongation only when the
  previous hits were round; a comet growing a tail is round first.

## What I want in the next scorecard

- Precision and recall at 3 and 4 hits, benchmark week, after items 1, 2, 7.
- The chance floor from item 8.
- Measured publication lag from item 4, from a day of live fetching.
- Status-at-k for every benchmark comet, so item 2 is visibly handled.
